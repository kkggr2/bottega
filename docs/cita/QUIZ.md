# Whose first line is it? (for Cita Press)

Seven openings from Cita Press's open library. Every quote is the first line of the book, checked against Cita's
own editions. Ready to paste into a newsletter or a post; it also plays on Bottega inside a Cita club
(bottegarooms.com/join/cita), with a score table.

1. "It is very seldom that mere ordinary people like John and myself secure ancestral halls for the summer."
   Charlotte Perkins Gilman · Kate Chopin · Edith Wharton · Mary Shelley
2. "A green and yellow parrot, which hung in a cage outside the door, kept repeating over and over: 'Allez vous-en! Allez vous-en! Sapristi! That's all right!'"
   Kate Chopin · Elizabeth Gaskell · Amy Lowell · Edith Wharton
3. "On the 22d of September I was asked by the World if I could have myself committed to one of the asylums for the insane in New York…"
   Nellie Bly · Harriet Jacobs · George Eliot · Charlotte Perkins Gilman
4. "I was born a slave; but I never knew it till six years of happy childhood had passed away."
   Harriet Jacobs · Zitkála-Šá · Sui Sin Far · Nellie Bly
5. "'Has she come?' 'No, Mamma, not yet.'"
   Louisa May Alcott · Elizabeth Gaskell · Edith Wharton · Kate Chopin
6. "It is only four o'clock; but it is winter and the sun has already set…"
   Mary Shelley · Virginia Woolf · Vernon Lee · Elizabeth Gaskell
7. "…a genus with many species, determined by the particular quality of silliness that predominates in them—the frothy, the prosy, the pious, or the pedantic."
   George Eliot · Virginia Woolf · Vernon Lee · Mary Shelley

**Answers:** 1 Gilman, *The Yellow Wall-Paper* (1892) · 2 Chopin, *The Awakening* (1899) · 3 Bly, *Ten Days in a
Mad-House* (1887) · 4 Jacobs, *Incidents in the Life of a Slave Girl* (1861) · 5 Alcott, *Behind a Mask* (1866) ·
6 Shelley, *Mathilda* (1819–20) · 7 Eliot, *Silly Novels by Lady Novelists* (1856). All free at citapress.org.
